# ADR-008 — Two languages and two unit systems, from the first release

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-10
- **Related:** INV-01, INV-02, INV-13, INV-19, INV-27, [09 §2](../09-business-model.md),
  [07 §9](../07-brand-and-ui.md)

## Context

Nothing in these documents specified a language. Every string, every exercise name and every email
was implicitly English — while the business model prices two markets, the United States and Brazil
([09 §2](../09-business-model.md)), and states that the developer is in Brazil and early users will
be too. The first paying market speaks Portuguese, and nothing in the system could speak to it.

Units had the opposite problem: they were specified, and specified wrongly for half the market.
`users.unit_system` existed and INV-01 correctly kept storage in SI, but three things were missing.

**Liftable loads were only liftable in kilograms.** INV-02 requires every prescribed load to be a
multiple of the exercise's increment, and every seeded increment was metric — barbell 2.5 kg. A
lifter in the United States loads 5 lb plates. Shown to them, a 42.5 kg prescription reads
**93.7 lb**: not a load anyone can put on a bar. That is the exact failure INV-02 exists to prevent,
arriving through the unit system instead of through arithmetic.

**Column precision would have broken imperial users even with the right increments.** A 5 lb
increment is 2.26796185 kg; `numeric(5,2)` stores it as 2.27. Simulated over a 52-cycle linear block
from 135 lb, with Postgres rounding applied on every write and `round_to_increment()` on every step,
**52 of 52 prescriptions land off the 5 lb grid — the first at cycle 1, as 140.1 lb.** At
`numeric(10,6)` for increments and `numeric(9,4)` for loads, the same simulation lands **0 of 52**
off-grid. This is a storage defect, not a display one, and it would have been invisible until an
American user opened the app.

**Distance and pace had no imperial form at all.** `pace_unit` knew `min_per_km` and `km_per_h`, and
auto-splits were fixed at 1 km and 5 km. A US runner would have been shown kilometres.

## Options considered

### Language

**A. English only for v1, Portuguese later.** Cheapest today. Rejected: the first paying market is
Brazilian, and extracting hardcoded strings after the fact is a known multi-week tax paid across
every screen, email and seeded row at once — the most expensive moment available.

**B. Portuguese only.** Matches the first users. Rejected: the US is priced as a primary market, and
the brand is English.

**C. Both from the first release, on an architecture where a third language is a file rather than a
project.** Chosen.

### Units

**A. Weight only (kg/lb), distance metric for everyone.** Rejected: a US runner reading kilometres is
reading an app that was not built for them.

**B. Two independent settings, weight and distance.** Covers the British runner who lifts in kg.
Rejected as the default: one more setting a beginner has to understand, for an edge case the
per-exercise increment override already half-covers.

**C. One setting, full metric/imperial.** Chosen.

## Decision

### Units

- **One setting — `users.unit_system`, `metric` | `imperial` — switches every presentation:** weight
  (kg/lb), distance (km/mi), pace and speed (min/km ↔ min/mi, km/h ↔ mph), elevation (m/ft),
  temperature (°C/°F) and body weight. **Storage stays SI**; INV-01 is unchanged in substance.
- **Liftable is defined in the unit the equipment is made in** (INV-02, extended). Imperial users get
  lb-native default increments, seeded beside the metric ones in `modality_increments`
  ([03 §2](../03-database-schema.md)) and stored as exact kg equivalents.
- **Rounding is unit-invariant — provided precision is sufficient.** Rounding to a multiple of an
  increment is a ratio, so it gives the same answer in kg and in lb *if* the stored increment is
  precise enough that one step cannot cross a half-increment boundary. `round_to_increment()`
  re-anchors every load to an exact multiple on every step, so error never accumulates. Increments are
  `numeric(10,6)`; loads are `numeric(9,4)`.
- **Switching unit system mid-block** changes presentation immediately and never touches history
  (INV-01). **Projected** microcycles re-project against the new unit's increments (INV-06), shown as
  a preview before the user commits — the same mechanism as a mid-block strategy switch (FR-3.6a).
  Completed and locked cycles do not move.
- **One setting drives both display and equipment**, accepted as a simplification. Someone who reads
  pounds but trains on kilogram plates overrides the increment per exercise.
- **Cardio.** Sport profiles carry a metric *and* an imperial pace unit and split distance. The GPS
  pipeline writes auto-splits in **both** bases at finish, so a unit switch recomputes nothing and
  INV-13's "reproduce exactly" stays true. **Swim pace follows the pool's unit, not the setting** — a
  25 yd pool is paced per 100 yd for everyone, because lengths are what a swimmer counts. **Rowing is
  `/500 m` universally**, because every erg monitor displays it that way.
- **Race distances are universal; round-number milestones are not.** A 5 k is a 5 k everywhere, and
  US runners call it that. "First 100 km month" and "First 100 mi month" are separate achievements,
  each shown to users of its own system; one already earned is never revoked by a later switch. The
  mile joins the running personal bests for everyone — it is a race distance in its own right.

### Language

- **English (`en`) and Brazilian Portuguese (`pt-BR`) from the first release.** `users.locale` stores
  the choice, because the API writes emails and exports and cannot see the device.
- **Language and unit system are independent.** An English speaker living in Brazil loads kilogram
  plates. Both default from the device at first launch — any Portuguese locale gives `pt-BR`,
  anything else `en`; a US region gives `imperial`, anything else `metric` — and both can be changed
  in settings at any time. Units are confirmed during onboarding; language is not asked, because the
  device has already answered it.
- **No user-facing string is hardcoded** (INV-27). Every string is a key into a message catalog.
- **Reference content is translated; user content never is.** Global exercise names, muscle groups,
  sports, session types, strokes, set types, achievements, explainers, emails, paywall, store
  listings and legal documents are translated. A custom exercise name, an activity title, a note or a
  routine name is shown exactly as the user wrote it, in whatever language they wrote it. A global
  exercise that the user forks becomes user content: its translated name is copied into the fork.
- **One set of catalogs, two consumers.** `packages/shared/i18n/en.json` and `pt-BR.json`, in ICU
  MessageFormat so plurals follow each language's rules. The app reads them for the UI; the API reads
  **the same files** for emails and exports. Reference rows in the database carry a stable **key** and
  no translated text, so the database holds no translations at all and a new language adds a file.
- **Search matches the translated name and the English one.** Brazilian gym vocabulary mixes the two
  freely — *supino* and *leg press* in the same sentence — so a pt-BR user must find an exercise by
  either.
- **Terminology is decided once, not by a translator screen by screen.** A glossary travels with the
  catalogs ([07 §9](../07-brand-and-ui.md)). RIR stays "RIR" in Portuguese, explained as *repetições
  em reserva*, because that is how Brazilian training practice uses it.

## Consequences

**Good**
- The first paying market uses the product in its own language on day one.
- An imperial user's prescriptions are liftable — demonstrated over a 52-cycle block, not assumed.
- A third language is a catalog file, a store listing and a legal review. No schema change, no code
  change.
- Keys in reference rows make the seed language-neutral: one catalog seed serves every locale.
- String fixes ship over OTA, because catalogs are JS-bundle data ([05 §9](../05-integrations.md)).

**Bad — accepted**
- **Every piece of reference content is written twice** — ~200 exercise names, every explainer and
  email, both store listings, the privacy policy and the terms. Real content work, and none of it can
  be deferred past launch.
- **The Portuguese legal documents must be reviewed against LGPD in their own right**, not merely
  translated from the English.
- **Portuguese runs roughly 20–30 % longer than English.** Every layout must reflow rather than
  truncate, and the set row must be tested in pt-BR at 200 % system font scale — the combination most
  likely to break it.
- **The visual test matrix doubles twice**: two languages × two unit systems, on top of two themes.
- One units setting means the reads-pounds-lifts-kilograms user configures increments by hand.
- Higher-precision numeric columns cost a few bytes per row. Irrelevant at this scale.

## Revisit if

- A third market is priced ([09 §6](../09-business-model.md)). It should be cheap by design; if it is
  not, that is a finding about this ADR.
- Users ask for separate weight and distance units in meaningful numbers. The answer is a second
  setting, not a reinterpretation of this one.
- A language with a non-Latin script or a right-to-left layout is added. That is layout work this
  decision does not cover.
