# ADR-006 — Subscription monetisation, and the line the paywall never crosses

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-09-08
- **Amends:** [ADR-005](ADR-005.md) (which assumed "free at launch, no in-app purchase")
- **Related:** INV-22, INV-26, [09-business-model.md](../09-business-model.md)

## Context

[ADR-005](ADR-005.md) built the gamification system on a stated assumption:

> **Assumed free at launch** with no in-app purchase, which is also the assumption that keeps
> ADR-005 honest — monetised engagement is what makes reward systems go bad.

That assumption is now void. CyberAthlete will be free for three months and paid thereafter, with
a discounted annual plan and a coach tier.

So ADR-005 has to be re-examined rather than quietly carried forward. The question is not "may we
charge" — of course we may. It is: **does charging money create pressure to break the invariants
that make the reward system safe?**

## The actual risk

The harm in monetised fitness apps does not come from charging. It comes from **revenue that
scales with time-in-app**:

| Model | What it rewards the business for | Pressure it creates |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising | Impressions → session count → daily opens | Notification spam, daily-login mechanics, streak anxiety |
| Consumable IAP | Purchases → loss aversion → urgency | Selling streak saves, XP boosts, "don't lose your progress" |
| Engagement-metered | Minutes in app | Manufactured incompleteness |
| **Flat subscription** | **Retention** | **Users getting results** |

Under the first three, INV-22 is in direct conflict with the P&L, and invariants that conflict with
revenue lose, eventually, quietly, at some future moment of commercial pressure.

Under a flat subscription, retention comes from users training well and staying. **The business
incentive and the user's interest point the same way.** That is the whole reason ADR-005 survives
here.

## Options considered

**A. Free, ad-supported.** Rejected outright. It would require reversing ADR-005 in substance
while pretending to keep it, and it would put an advertising SDK next to GPS traces
([04 §6](../04-security-and-auth.md)).

**B. Freemium with consumable IAP.** Rejected. The natural things to sell in a gamified app are
exactly the things INV-22 forbids.

**C. Flat subscription, planner behind the paywall, logging free.** Chosen.

**D. Paid up front, no free tier.** Honest and simple, but nobody buys a training app they have not
trained with, and a 12-cycle block cannot be evaluated in a store listing.

## Decision

**Option C**, with the tier structure in [09 §1](../09-business-model.md), plus a hard boundary:

### INV-26 — The paywall never touches the user's own data or their safety
Permanently free at every tier, including an expired one:

- reading, charting and **exporting** one's own training history;
- **logging a workout or recording an activity** — the core loop always works;
- multi-device sync of one's own data;
- account deletion and every privacy control;
- the cardio volume safety rails (FR-6.5a).

A lapsed subscription **downgrades features, never revokes data.** An in-progress mesocycle becomes
read-only; it is never deleted and never hidden.

*Why:* holding somebody's three years of training history hostage to a payment is indefensible,
and "your data is always yours" is the promise that makes a fitness subscription trustworthy. It is
also the promise most often broken in this category.

### Three months, no card
The trial is three months because **a training block is 8–12 microcycles** — that is the shortest
window in which the product can demonstrate its thesis. No card is taken, so nobody is ever charged
by surprise; at month three the account simply becomes Free.

This converts worse than a card-on-file trial. Accepted deliberately: a surprise charge is the
single fastest way to make a user distrust an app they have given their training history to.

### The pre-existing ban is reaffirmed, not relaxed
No streak protection, XP, levels, or achievements may **ever** be purchased (INV-22,
[08 §6](../08-gamification.md)). **Money buys features. It never buys progress.**

## Consequences

**Good**
- ADR-005's invariants stay compatible with the business rather than in tension with it.
- No advertising SDK ever comes near a GPS trace.
- The free tier is genuinely useful, not deliberately crippled, so the app is defensible as a
  recommendation even to someone who will never pay.
- Unit economics are comfortable: ~$55 net on an annual plan against ~$0.30–1.00/year of storage
  ([06 §9](../06-operations.md)). The binding constraint is acquisition and support, not servers.

**Bad — accepted**
- **Lower conversion**, from both the no-card trial and the generous free tier. This is the second
  time this project has knowingly traded engagement for integrity (the first was ADR-005). Both
  trades should be revisited on evidence, never on pressure.
- Three months of full-cost usage per user before any revenue, and some users never convert.
  Cheap at this scale; worth re-checking if acquisition ever becomes paid.
- **The planner being paid couples the business model to the gamification design** — Free users
  have no prescription, so their Consistency and Recovery tracks barely move
  ([09 §4](../09-business-model.md)). The upsell is real and legitimate; making it *feel* punishing
  would be a dark pattern. State the reason, never manufacture the feeling.
- The Coach tier breaks single-owner data ownership and needs its own permission model and its own
  security review. It is v1.1 ([task 015](../tasks/015-coach-tier.md)), not a launch feature.

## Revisit if

- Conversion is so low the project cannot sustain itself. The response is to reconsider **what is
  in Pro**, or the price — never to start selling progress, and never to move an INV-26 item behind
  the paywall.
- The Coach tier changes the economics enough to warrant a different structure for it specifically.
